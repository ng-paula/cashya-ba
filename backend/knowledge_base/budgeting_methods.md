# Budgeting Methods Guide

> Sample knowledge-base document for the Cashya Ba? prototype. Content summarizes widely used
> personal-finance methods. Review against your chosen references (e.g. BSP financial education materials) before the defense.

## The 50/30/20 rule

The 50/30/20 rule splits take-home income into three parts: 50% for needs (food, rent, transport, bills),
30% for wants (eating out, shopping, milk tea, streaming), and 20% for savings or paying debt.
Example: on a ₱20,000 sahod, about ₱10,000 goes to needs, ₱6,000 to wants, and ₱4,000 to savings.

## The 70/20/10 rule for tight budgets

When income is small, like a student's baon or a minimum-wage salary, needs often take more than half.
A common alternative is 70/20/10: 70% needs, 20% savings, and 10% wants.
If even that is impossible, start by saving any fixed small amount (₱20 a day is ₱600 a month) and build the habit first.

## Envelope (sobre) method

The envelope method divides cash into labeled envelopes or wallets: food, pamasahe, load, wants.
When an envelope is empty, spending in that category stops until the next baon or sahod.
Digital version: e-wallet "pockets" or separate accounts, or category limits in Cashya Ba?.

## Pay yourself first

Set aside savings on day 1 of receiving money, before spending on anything else.
Saving "whatever is left" at the end usually means saving nothing, because spending expands to fill the budget.

## Daily allowance method

Divide the money you can spend by the number of days until the next baon or sahod.
Example: ₱7,700 for 15 days is about ₱513 per day. If you spend less today, the extra is spread
over the remaining days; if you overspend, the following days get a smaller allowance.
This is the method Cashya Ba? uses.
